# WeBuildit — Origin Story

> [!NOTE]
> **Story lane:** Origin / founder journey  
> **Status:** Living narrative source  
> **Use:** Website About, founder posts, interviews, community onboarding, future video/script material

## The short version

WeBuildit did not begin with AI.

It began with systems, websites, people working together, and the question: **what could we build if each person brought a different strength?**

The technical path moved through system administration, Drupal, collaborative development processes, DevOps, automation and eventually AI-assisted development.

The original idea was already collaborative. AI did not invent that idea — AI made it possible to pursue it with a much smaller and more capable team.

## Where the path started

Ja Bular worked as a **system administrator** in a company whose work was strongly connected to Drupal-based web development. During that period the environment exposed him not only to servers and websites, but also to the way a larger development organization operates: shared workflows, Jira, specialized roles, delivery processes and the gradual rise of the word and discipline **DevOps**.

The company later became part of / operated under **FFW**, a larger international Drupal-focused organization.

The important part of this chapter was not a job title. It was the direction it created:

> systems are not isolated machines; software, infrastructure, processes and people form one delivery system.

## After the job

After leaving the company, that direction stayed.

Ja continued building his own websites and independently studying the areas that had opened up through the work experience. DevOps became a natural continuation: infrastructure, deployment, automation, containers, operations, reliability and the question of how to make systems easier to build and maintain.

Learning continued through online courses and professional learning platforms, including LinkedIn Learning and other sources.

This phase gradually moved the identity from:

> **I maintain systems**

toward:

> **I want to understand how the whole system is built.**

## The first WeBuildit idea

The early WeBuildit concept was a collaborative development team / coworking model.

The idea was simple:

- several people with different strengths;
- each person owns the part they understand best;
- the team takes real projects together;
- members learn from one another;
- projects create income;
- reusable knowledge stays with the team;
- over time the team becomes capable of solving larger problems.

It was never meant to be only one person selling websites.

The name carried the idea from the beginning:

> **We build it.**

## The AI turning point

AI changed the economics of the original idea.

Historically, a serious digital project might require many separate roles: development, operations, testing, architecture, documentation, project coordination, research, design and marketing.

Today a capable human working with multiple AI systems can cover a much larger part of that surface. A small team of such people can operate with a level of leverage that previously required a much larger organization.

This creates a new version of WeBuildit:

> **human direction + AI capability + shared knowledge + real implementation**

AI is not the mission. **Building useful things is the mission.** AI is one of the most powerful tools now available for doing that.

## What “we” means now

The word **we** has expanded.

It can mean:

- Ja + another developer;
- a small project team;
- a client working closely with builders;
- a distributed group of specialists;
- humans + AI assistants and coding agents;
- a wider learning and building community.

A modern WeBuildit workflow may include different AI systems for different roles: strategic thinking, repository analysis, implementation, browser operation, research, review, documentation or quality assurance — while humans retain direction, responsibility and judgment.

## The current chapter

The current transition is:

**System Administration → Web / Drupal → DevOps → Automation → AI-assisted Development → AI-native Building**

This is not presented as a finished destination. It is the chapter being lived now.

The most credible content should therefore come from current work:

- how AI is actually used in projects;
- where it saves time;
- where it fails;
- how multiple AI tools can work as a team;
- how governance and review prevent chaos;
- what ordinary businesses can practically adopt;
- how technical infrastructure changes when AI becomes part of everyday work.

## Core founder-story line

A reusable line for future posts/interviews:

> **I started by maintaining systems. Then I wanted to understand how systems are built. DevOps taught me to connect software, infrastructure and process. Now AI is changing the way we build again — and WeBuildit is my place to explore that change in real projects, together with people and AI.**

## Story rule

Keep this story grounded in chronology and evidence.

Do not rewrite the past to make it look like AI was always the plan.

The power of the story is exactly the opposite:

> one technical direction naturally led to the next, and the meaning of WeBuildit became clearer as the tools evolved.
